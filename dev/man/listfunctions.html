Default viscosity law of the convecting regions: the hydrous rhyolitic melt of Giordano et al. (2008) (72 wt % SiO₂, 6 wt % H₂O) with the crystal correction of Costa et al. (2009), i.e. a GeoParams creep law that is evaluated with the temperature and melt fraction of the region.

MagmaThermoKinematics.EruptionParams Type
julia
EruptionParams(; kwargs...)

Parameters of the chamber/eruption model. Pressures in Pa, lengths in m, times in s.

  • ϕ_erupt: minimum melt fraction of a mobile chamber cell

  • h_melt_min: minimum melt thickness (∫ϕ dz equivalent) for a region to count as a chamber [m]

  • ΔP_crit, ΔP_relax: overpressure at which the chamber drains, and the one it relaxes to

  • ϕ_g_crit: gas volume fraction above which the chamber is gas-locked and cannot erupt

  • σ_barrier: stress barrier that the dike has to overcome [Pa]

  • w_dike, κ_magma: dike half-width [m] and magma thermal diffusivity [m²/s] (dike freezing)

  • μ_shear: host-rock shear modulus [Pa]; ν: Poisson ratio used for the displacement field

  • A_visc, G_act: prefactor [Pa s] and activation energy [J/mol] of the Arrhenius viscosity η = A_visc exp(G_act/(R T)) of the host rock

  • shell_rheology: nothing (default: the Arrhenius law above) or a GeoParams creep law of the host rock (e.g. SetDislocationCreep(GeoParams.Dislocation.granite_Tirel_2008), any law with compute_εII(law, τ, (; T, P))), evaluated at the local stress of the shell τ(s) = 1.5 ΔP / s³ (s = r/R, ΔP the overpressure, at least shell_ΔP_min) and the lithostatic pressure

  • shell_from_T: take the temperature of the shell around the chamber from the simulated temperature field instead of the steady conduction profile to the undisturbed geotherm (default)

  • shell_ratio, shell_nθ: outer radius of the shell in chamber radii, and number of rays from the chamber centre along which the shell is sampled (in the section in 2D, over the sphere in 3D)

  • η_shell_min, η_shell_max: bounds of the relaxation viscosity of the shell [Pa s]

  • m_w: total H₂O mass fraction of the magma

  • ρ_melt, ρ_x, crust: densities as (ρ0, α, β) (reference density, expansivity, compressibility)

  • melt_viscosity, solubility, ρ_gas: GeoParams laws

  • g: gravity

MagmaThermoKinematics.NumParam Type

This mutable structure represents numerical parameters in the program. It is used to store and manage numerical values that are used throughout the program. mutable struct NumParam <: NumericalParameters

Fields

  • SimName::String: Name of the simulation.

  • FigTitle: Title of the figure.

  • Nx::Int64: Number of grid points in the x direction.

  • Nz::Int64: Number of grid points in the z direction.

  • W::Float64: Width of the domain.

  • H::Float64: Height of the domain.

  • dx::Float64: Grid spacing in the x direction.

  • dz::Float64: Grid spacing in the z direction.

  • refine::NamedTuple: refine the grid in a zone of interest while keeping Nx, Ny, Nz (default (;), constant spacing): an entry per refined direction (x, y, z) with the keyword arguments of refined_coordinates, e.g. refine = (x = (zone = (0.0, 5e3), ratio = 4), z = (zone = (-10e3, -4e3), ratio = 4)). The spacing in the zones is ratio times smaller than outside. The time step is then computed from the smallest spacing (unless dt is given explicitly), and dx, dy, dz and Δ hold the smallest spacing per direction once MTK_GeoParams has created the grid.

  • Tsurface_Celsius::Float64: Surface temperature in Celsius.

  • sticky_air_BC::Bool: hold every cell of phase AirPhase at Tsurface_Celsius at the end of each time step (default false). The usual alternative, a very large air conductivity, makes the air the fastest-diffusing phase and so shrinks the time step of the whole model (see bc_sticky_air!).

  • AirPhase::Int64: phase number of the air (default 0), used by sticky_air_BC, move_free_surface, convection and out_of_plane.

  • Geotherm::Float64: Geothermal gradient in K/m.

  • maxTime_Myrs::Float64: Maximum simulation time in Myrs.

  • maxTime::Float64: Maximum simulation time in seconds.

  • SaveOutput_steps::Int64: Number of steps between output saves.

  • CreateFig_steps::Int64: Number of steps between figure creations.

  • flux_bottom_BC::Bool: Whether to apply a flux at the bottom boundary.

  • flux_bottom::Float64: Flux at the bottom boundary in W/m^2.

  • plot_tracers::Bool: Whether to plot tracers.

  • advect_polygon::Bool: Whether to advect a polygon around the intrusion area.

  • axisymmetric::Bool: Whether the simulation is axisymmetric.

  • κ_time::Float64: Thermal diffusivity.

  • fac_dt::Float64: Factor to multiply the time step by.

  • dt::Float64: Time step.

  • time::Float64: Current time.

  • nt::Int64: Total number of time steps.

  • it::Int64: Current iteration.

  • convection::Bool: parameterised convection (default false): the conductivity of every connected region with melt fraction ≥ convection_phi is multiplied by the Nusselt number of its Rayleigh number (log10 Nu quadratic in log10 Ra with coefficients convection_coeffs, Nu = 1 below convection_Ra_crit, bounded by convection_Nu_max); needs solver = :implicit_enthalpy. convection_alpha, convection_viscosity (a GeoParams creep law) and convection_strainrate set the scaling; convection_viscosity may be a tuple of laws with convection_z_split the depths separating them (chambers of different composition), see compute_convective_nusselt!.

  • out_of_plane::Bool: heat loss along the out-of-plane direction of a planar 2D model (default false), -factor K (T - Tbg)/L²(z) with L²(z) the horizontal variance of the anomaly T - Tbg (Arrays.Tbg, by default the initial temperature) at each depth.

  • out_of_plane_factor::Float64, out_of_plane_smooth::Int64, out_of_plane_Lmin::Float64: prefactor, averaging half-width in depth cells and lower bound [m] of the length scale.

  • ω::Float64: Relaxation parameter for nonlinear iterations (default 0.5; values ≥ 0.7 may not converge at high resolution).

  • seed::Union{Nothing, Int64}: seed of the random number generator at the start of a simulation (reproducible random sills); nothing leaves the generator unchanged.

  • print_injections::Bool: print a line for every injected dike/sill (default true).

  • move_free_surface::Bool: move the free surface (air/rock boundary) with the host-rock displacement of every injection (default false).

  • Output_VTK::Bool: write VTK output; the output directory SimName is only created if this is true.

  • solver::Symbol: :explicit (default) keeps the existing explicit step. :implicit_enthalpy solves a backward-Euler step of the enthalpy balance, linearised with Newton iterations and solved with an accelerated pseudo-transient iteration (see apt_diffusion_solve!); the time step factor fac_dt can then be much larger than 1. :apt is an alias.

  • apt_tol, apt_max_iter, apt_check: tolerance, iteration limit and check interval of the pseudo-transient iterations (implicit enthalpy solver).

  • max_iter::Int64: Maximum number of nonlinear iterations.

  • verbose::Bool: Whether to print verbose output.

  • convergence::Float64: Convergence criterion for nonlinear iterations.

  • deactivate_La_at_depth::Bool: Whether to deactivate latent heating at the bottom of the model box.

  • deactivationDepth::Float64: Depth at which to deactivate latent heating.

  • backend: KernelAbstractions backend on which the model arrays live: CPU() (default), or e.g. CUDABackend() once CUDA.jl is loaded.

  • FloatType: Element type of the model arrays (default Float64).

  • AnalyticalInitialGeo::Bool: Whether to use an analytical initial geotherm.

  • qs_anal::Float64: Analytical surface heat flux.

  • qm_anal::Float64: Analytical mantle heat flux.

  • hr_anal::Float64: Analytical radiogenic heat production.

  • k_anal::Float64: Analytical thermal conductivity.

  • InitialEllipse::Bool: Whether to initialize with an ellipse.

  • a_init::Float64: Semi-major axis of initial ellipse.

  • b_init::Float64: Semi-minor axis of initial ellipse.

  • TrackTracersOnGrid::Bool: Whether to track tracers on the grid.

  • TtPath_steps::Int64: Number of time steps between samples of the tracer temperature–time paths (time_vec, T_vec).

Examples

julia

MagmaThermoKinematics.AdvectTemperature! Method
julia
AdvectTemperature!(Tnew, T, Grid, Velocity, dt)

Semi-Lagrangian advection of T by the displacement field Velocity (one array per direction, in meters, defined on the grid nodes) over pseudo-time dt, into Tnew. Grid is the tuple of 1D coordinate ranges. With constant spacing, every departure point is index arithmetic; on a refined grid (coordinate vectors) its cell is found by bisection. The RK2 velocity sample and the temperature read are multilinear. This is the RK2/linear scheme of AdvectTemperature, as a kernel on the backend of T.

Tnew must not alias T: the departure point of one node generally reads values other nodes still need.

MagmaThermoKinematics.MTK_GeoParams Method
julia
Grid, Arrays, Tracers, Dikes, time_props = MTK_GeoParams(Mat_tup::Tuple, Num::NumericalParameters, Dikes::SillParameters; CartData_input=nothing, time_props::TimeDependentProperties = TimeDepProps());

Main routine that performs a 2D, 2D axisymmetric or 3D thermal diffusion simulation with injection of dikes. The model is 3D if Num.Ny > 0 (or if CartData_input is 3D), and 2D otherwise. The model arrays live on the KernelAbstractions backend Num.backend, with element type Num.FloatType.

Parameters

  • Mat_tup::Tuple: Tuple of material properties.

  • Num::NumericalParameters: Numerical parameters.

  • Dikes::SillParameters: Intrusion parameters.

  • CartData_input::CartData: Optional input of a CartData structure generated with GeophysicalModelGenerator.

  • time_props::TimeDependentProperties: Optional input of a TimeDependentProperties structure.

  • callbacks::NamedTuple: Optional per-simulation replacements of the customizable functions below, e.g. callbacks = (; print_output = my_print, update_TimeDepProps! = my_props!); the keys are the function names without the MTK_ prefix and the arguments are the same. Callbacks only affect this simulation, so several simulations with different callbacks can run in one session, also concurrently.

Customizable functions

There are a few functions that you can overwrite in your user code (or replace for a single simulation with callbacks) to customize the simulation:

  • MTK_visualize_output(Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Arrays::NamedTuple, Mat_tup::Tuple, Dikes::SillParameters)

  • MTK_update_TimeDepProps!(time_props::TimeDependentProperties, Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Arrays::NamedTuple, Mat_tup::Tuple, Dikes::SillParameters)

  • MTK_update_ArraysStructs!(Arrays::NamedTuple, Grid::GridData, Dikes::SillParameters, Num::NumericalParameters, Mat_tup::Tuple)

  • MTK_initialize!(Arrays::NamedTuple, Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Tracers::StructArray, Dikes::SillParameters, CartData_input)

  • MTK_updateTracers(Grid::GridData, Arrays::NamedTuple, Tracers::StructArray, Dikes::SillParameters, time_props::TimeDependentProperties, Num::NumericalParameters)

  • MTK_save_output(Grid::GridData, Arrays::NamedTuple, Tracers::StructArray, Dikes::SillParameters, time_props::TimeDependentProperties, Num::NumericalParameters, CartData_input::CartData)

  • MTK_inject_dikes(Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Arrays::NamedTuple, Mat_tup::Tuple, Dikes::SillParameters, Tracers::StructVector)

  • MTK_source!(Arrays::NamedTuple, Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Mat_tup::Tuple, Dikes::SillParameters)

  • MTK_initialize!(Arrays::NamedTuple, Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Tracers::StructArray, Dikes::SillParameters)

  • MTK_finalize!(Arrays::NamedTuple, Grid::GridData, Num::NumericalParameters, Tracers::StructArray, Dikes::SillParameters, CartData_input::CartData)

MagmaThermoKinematics.bc_sticky_air! Method
julia
bc_sticky_air!(T, Phases, AirPhase, Tair)

Hold every cell of phase AirPhase at the temperature Tair.

The air above the topography is a numerical device that keeps the ground surface at a prescribed temperature. Emulating that with a very high air conductivity makes the air the fastest-diffusing phase of the model, and since the time step is set by the largest diffusivity anywhere (κ_time), the whole domain is then advanced with a time step that is one to three orders of magnitude smaller than the rock requires. MagmaThermoKinematics.inject_sills Method
julia
Tracers, Tnew, InjectedVolume, dike_poly, Velocity =
    inject_sills(Tracers, T, Grid, sill, T_in, Phase_in, nTr_dike;
                 AdvectionMethod="RK2", InterpolationMethod="Linear",
                 dike_poly=[])

Inject a sill/dike described by the InjectSills.AbstractSill object sill into the temperature field T defined on the grid Grid (constant spacing or refined).

Arguments

  • Tracers – StructArray of Tracer objects (may be unassigned on first call)

  • T – temperature array [°C], mutated in-place; any backend

  • Grid – 1-D coordinate vectors (x, z) in 2-D or (x, y, z) in 3-D (ranges, or vectors for a refined grid)

  • sill – AbstractSill (e.g. PennyShapedSill) with the desired center, orientation, size, and elastic parameters already set

  • T_in – temperature of the injected magma [°C]

  • Phase_in – rock-phase index assigned to new tracers

  • nTr_dike – number of new tracers to seed inside the sill

Keyword arguments

  • AdvectionMethod – "RK2" (default) or "Euler"

  • InterpolationMethod – "Linear", "Quadratic", or "Cubic" (default "Linear")

  • dike_poly – optional plotting polygon (InjectSills.dike_polygon) that is advected with the host rock

Returns

(Tracers, Tnew, InjectedVolume, dike_poly, Velocity), where InjectedVolume is the equivalent 3D volume of sill in m³ (InjectSills.volume).

Algorithm

The temperature field is advected by the displacement field of InjectSills.hostrock_displacement! over nsteps pseudo-time steps, so that the displacement per step stays below half the smallest grid spacing. MagmaThermoKinematics.mogi_displacement! Method
julia
mogi_displacement!(U, X, centers, ΔV; L=zeros, ν=0.25, softening)

Displacement U (tuple of arrays, one per direction) at the node coordinates X (tuple of arrays) due to Mogi point sources at centers (Ns × N) with volume changes ΔV (Ns; positive = inflation, negative = withdrawal). Each source contributes (1−ν) ΔV/π · d/|d|³, which is the InjectSills.MogiSphere solution written in terms of its volume change ΔV = π ΔP r³/G; the contributions are summed (linear elasticity). In a 2D section a source with L[s] > 0 is instead a line of point sources along the out-of-plane direction over [-L, L], with ΔV the volume change per unit length: the integrated kernel is 2L · d/(|d|² √(|d|² + L²)). The sum runs on the backend of X[1]; softening [m] regularises the singularity at the sources' own nodes. With halfspace = :image the sources are volume-consistent dilatation centres (ΔV/(4π) · d/|d|³, whose divergence integrates to ΔV) with opposite-sign images mirrored at z_surface (surface uplift twice the full-space value); halfspace = :mindlin (3-D point sources) is the exact half-space solution of Mindlin & Cheng (1950) — volume change ΔV in the bulk and the Mogi displacement at the free surface z_surface; the :mogi kernel is the surface formula of the half-space solution and over-dilates the interior by 4(1 − ν). The half-space kernels assume a flat free surface at z_surface and are not valid at nodes above it; keep z_surface at or above the highest rock surface (see erupt!).

The returned centers (Ns × 3, relative to the axis) and ΔV (negative, in m³) are to be used with receivers on the section through the axis, (x - x_axis, 0, z), and mogi_displacement! with halfspace = :mindlin. With symmetric = true (and an even nθ) only the azimuths in (0, π) are summed, with twice the volume (exact for receivers on the section).

MagmaThermoKinematics.Grid.CreateGrid Method

Creates a 1D, 2D or 3D grid of given size. Grid can be created by defining the size and either the extent (length) of the grid in all directions, or by defining start & end points

Spacing is constant, unless the grid is refined with refine or given by its coordinates with coords.

Note: since this is mostly for Solid Earth geoscience applications, in 2D the second dimension is called z (vertical)

Refined grids

With refine, the points of a direction are concentrated in a zone of interest (e.g. around a magma chamber) while their number stays the same: refine is a named tuple with an entry per refined direction (x, y, z; in 2D x and z), each holding the keyword arguments of refined_coordinates, e.g.

julia
Grid = CreateGrid(size=(301, 301), extent=(20e3, 20e3), refine=(x = (zone = (0.0, 5e3), ratio = 4), z = (zone = (-10e3, -4e3), ratio = 4)))

gives a spacing 4 times smaller inside the zones than outside, with a smooth transition. Alternatively, coords gives the 1D coordinate vectors of all directions, e.g. coords = (x, z). The grid is then a tensor product of 1D coordinate vectors (Grid.ConstantΔ == false, Grid.coord1D holds Vectors and Grid.Δ the smallest spacing per direction); all directions are stored as vectors if one of them is non-uniform.

MagmaThermoKinematics.Grid.refined_coordinates Method
julia
x = refined_coordinates(lo, hi, n; zone, ratio = 4.0, Δ = nothing, transition = nothing, max_growth = 1.05)

n coordinates from lo to hi that are concentrated in the interval zone = (a, b): the spacing is constant inside the zone and grows smoothly (as 1 - cos) over the distance transition outside it, to ratio times the spacing in the zone. Instead of ratio, the spacing in the zone can be given with Δ. By default transition is chosen such that the spacing of neighbouring points differs by at most a factor max_growth (the spacing reaches ratio times that of the zone only if the domain is long enough for this transition). The spacing of the zone follows from n: compared with the constant spacing (hi - lo)/(n - 1), a refined direction resolves the zone better with the same number of points. The smallest spacing that can be reached this way is limited (a longer transition costs points); a larger max_growth or a given transition allows stronger refinement.

With transition = 0 the spacing changes abruptly at the edges of the zone, which are then grid points: constant spacing in the zone and (about ratio times larger) constant spacing outside it. No points are spent on a transition, so the zone is resolved best for a given number of points; the discretisation stays conservative across the jump.
