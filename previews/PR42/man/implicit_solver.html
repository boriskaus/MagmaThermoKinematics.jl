Implicit Enthalpy Solver ​

By default, the diffusion step of MagmaThermoKinematics is explicit (see Numerics and Physics), and its time step is bounded by the stability limit  . With NumParam(solver = :implicit_enthalpy) (alias :apt) the diffusion and latent-heat step is instead solved implicitly, as a backward-Euler step of the enthalpy balance. The time step is then limited only by accuracy, so fac_dt can be much larger than 1. This pays off for long simulations, fine grids and strongly temperature-dependent latent heat. The implicit step runs on every KernelAbstractions backend (CPU, CUDA, ...), in 2D, axisymmetric and 3D models, and in single or double precision.

Usage ​

julia
using MagmaThermoKinematics

Num = NumParam(
    Nx = 269, Nz = 269, maxTime_Myrs = 1.0,
    solver = :implicit_enthalpy,   # or :apt
    fac_dt = 4.0,                  # time step = fac_dt · Δ²/κ/4; the explicit step needs fac_dt ≲ 0.5
    ω = 1.0,                       # relaxation of the nonlinear (outer) iterations
    apt_tol = 1.0e-6,              # tolerance of the pseudo-transient (inner) iterations
)

Everything else, including the material parameters, intrusions, tracers and output, is unchanged. The step is also available directly through Nonlinear_Diffusion_step!(Arrays, Mat_tup, Phases, Grid, dt, Num) when Num.solver is :implicit_enthalpy. MTK_GeoParams creates the work arrays it needs (pt_C, pt_b, pt_phiold, pt_res, pt_Tk, pt_Tkm1, pt_Tn).

Boundary conditions and geometry ​

The boundary conditions are those of the explicit step: zero heat flux on the lateral faces, and on the bottom either the temperature of the previous time level (isothermal, the default) or a prescribed heat flux (flux_bottom_BC = true, flux_bottom). Axisymmetric models (axisymmetric = true) use the cylindrical face weights in the radial direction, and 3D models the 7-point stencil. Material properties are evaluated at cell centres, and the conductivity of a face is the arithmetic mean of the two adjacent cells, as in the explicit step.

Accuracy and choice of the time step ​

The implicit step is unconditionally stable, but it is first order in time like the explicit one, so the time step still controls the accuracy. The relevant time scale is the cooling time of the smallest thermal structure that matters, for example   for an intrusion of thickness . The time step should be a small fraction of it.

As an example, a 500 m thick sill at 1000 °C (with latent heat, 4th-order melting curve) cools in a 20 km × 20 km box on a 201 × 201 grid (Δ = 100 m,    m²/s,   kyr). This is the maximum temperature difference to an explicit reference (fac_dt = 0.4, Δt = 28 yr) after 3 kyr:

The error grows roughly linearly with Δt. fac_dt of a few to about 10 keeps it to a few kelvin for such intrusions. Larger values are fine when only the long-term thermal state of the crust matters, for example in a spin-up phase. The gain over the explicit step grows with the resolution, because the explicit time step shrinks with , while the accuracy limit of the implicit step does not depend on the grid.

Verification ​

test/test_PseudoTransient.jl checks the implicit step against direct (dense) solves of the same discretisation (constant and variable conductivity, source terms, latent heat; 2D, 3D and axisymmetric), steady states with a source term, the agreement of the explicit and implicit steps for small time steps, the relaxation term, and the growth of the iteration count with the time step.
